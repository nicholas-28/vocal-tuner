# VOCAL TUNER — LEARNING EXPERIENCE ARCHITECTURE SYNTHESIS v1.0

Inputs read in full: CANONICAL RESEARCH EVIDENCE BASE v1.0 (C001–C055, H001–H015, L1–L4, Graveyard, Product Firewall, Transfer & Retention Ledger, E-A…E-G, Decision Dependency Graph, §9 safe-to-build) and VOCAL_TUNER_MECHANICS_DISCOVERY_PASS_v1 (M01–M48, N01–N16, R01–R05, families F1–F13, primitives, anti-patterns, §20 research mapping). No new research was conducted; two product facts from the discovery pass are reused where a contradiction needed resolving.

Labels used: **SAFE** (useful under all competing canonical models), **EXPERIMENTAL** (useful, but tests a named open hypothesis), **BLOCKED** (needs evidence before learner-facing use). Progression dimensions additionally use SUPPORTED / PLAUSIBLE. Confidence is stated where a design judgement rather than evidence decides.

## 0. THE SYSTEM IN ONE PAGE

**Product thesis.** Vocal Tuner becomes a place where the voice is played rather than measured: every attempt is a short vocal action (find, hold, step, keep, mirror, answer, judge, roam) posed by a musical partner, rendered as a musical consequence instead of a number, attached to an item that carries an honest state (never heard / heard once / practised / dark / due tomorrow), and remembered by a map that shows what the voice can return to and what has faded. The tuner's precision stays underneath as description and research; what the learner meets is relation, arrival, holding, returning, remembering and answering. Learning is claimed nowhere; retention is *asked* every day by the partner and the map, and that daily asking is simultaneously the game's progression currency and the missing experiment (C028, E-A).

**What the layered hypothesis got right and wrong.** "Instrument = moment, Conversation = session, Memory = spine, World = long-term, Ledger = invisible" survives as a list of *components*, not as a stack of timescales (§1). Every single attempt already touches all five: an action (grammar) rendered by an instrument-style consequence, posed by a partner, on an item with a memory state, at a place, logged. Timescales are what those components *produce* when repeated; they are not layers. The corrected architecture is **Attempt = ACTION × CONSEQUENCE × PARTNER × ITEM-STATE × PLACE, logged** (§10.15 diagram).

**The four architectures collapse to one plus two forks.** Instrument-first (A) is the renderer; Conversation-first (B) is the poser; Memory-first (C) is the item spine; World-first (D) is the persistence view. Choosing among them by the ten criteria (§10) selects C as the spine because it is the only one whose native currency is the missing data, B as the default poser because it scales from a tone to a phrase without changing the loop, A as the mandatory renderer because it is the only feedback channel the firewall does not restrict, and D as a deferred visualisation of the ledger. Two forks stay open on evidence, not taste: *earned vs scheduled fading* (H009) and *words-first vs notes-first grain* (H003/E-B); one fork stays open on prototype: *resonance vs harmony-following* as the primary consequence (K1).

**Minimum lovable core.** MVP-0 (1–2 days): KEEP + CALL-IT on the existing keyboard reference and settled-center evidence — hear, wait, sing, predict, coarse reveal, a chord blooms on arrival; all attempts logged. MVP-1 (~1 week): the Ringing Room — resonators as the consequence, items with onsight/flash/redpoint/dark state, expanding delays, a blind opening block next day, capture/export. MVP-2 (~1 month): words and a partner — two-degree vocabulary, three-note words, complete-the-phrase, assigned reference and fading conditions per item, the map as ledger view, ghosts from capture, teacher view.

**Blocked by evidence, not by effort:** cents-tier scoring, stability, onset and vibrato scoring, global scores, mastery labels, drone curricula, adaptive difficulty from per-frame cents, song-transfer promises, phenotype routing, any center definition presented as "the pitch you sang".

## 1. ATTACK ON THE LAYERED-TIMESCALE HYPOTHESIS

Hypothesis under attack: *Moment = Instrument; Session = Conversation; Spine = Memory/Retention; Long-term = World; Invisible = Measurement.*

**Attack 1 — the layers are not timescales.** A conversation has a 3-second loop (question → answer); memory has a 3-second loop (hear → wait → sing) and a 7-day loop; the instrument has a month-long loop (what you can play). Assigning each identity a timescale is a presentation choice that would force modes ("now you are in Conversation mode"). Verdict: reject the timescale mapping.

**Attack 2 — "Memory as spine" can become an exam spine.** If the spine is "retention tests", every session ends in a test and the product becomes the laboratory it is trying not to be. Verdict: the spine must be *item state*, not tests. An item's state (never heard / heard once / practised / dark / due) drives what the partner asks, and a blind probe is simply "the partner asks for something whose state says it is due" — one to three per session, never at the end, never scored aloud.

**Attack 3 — "Instrument" and "items" contradict.** An instrument has no items; it is played freely. A spine of items forbids free play. Verdict: both are needed and they coexist as two request sources: the partner poses items (spine) and the learner may ROAM at any time (instrument); roaming is logged descriptively and never carries item state. If free play is removed the identity collapses into drills; if items are removed the identity collapses into a toy (M20 lesson).

**Attack 4 — the world may be lore.** A persistent world is valuable only if every object *is* a ledger entry rendered spatially (an anchor, an item state, a range boundary, a condition, a take). Verdict: the world is the ledger's view, built last, and every object must pass the six-way test in §7.

**Attack 5 — "invisible measurement" can silently drive the game.** If measurement is invisible but *drives* difficulty, the learner is being adapted to by numbers the firewall forbids (per-frame cents), i.e., the meter is back, hidden. Verdict: measurement drives only *assignment* (which condition an item gets) and *state transitions* on coarse feedback-off repeats; it never drives moment-to-moment difficulty.

**Attack 6 — five components is a lot to build.** Verdict: true; the MVP ladder (§9) builds them in dependency order — action + consequence (MVP-0), item state (MVP-1), partner (MVP-2), place (after), and the ledger from day one because it is the cheapest and the only irreplaceable one.

**Reformulated hypothesis (accepted):** one loop, five components, present in every attempt:

```
ATTEMPT = ACTION (grammar primitive + modifiers)
        × CONSEQUENCE (how the outcome is rendered: musical, world, informational, corrective, reflective)
        × PARTNER (who poses the problem: partner / place / learner / peer)
        × ITEM-STATE (what the item knows about itself: information condition, delay schedule, dark/lit, novelty)
        × PLACE (where it persists: the map)
        → LEDGER (what is logged: attempt, condition, coarse outcome, versioned centers)
```

Timescales fall out: moment = ACTION × CONSEQUENCE; session = a PARTNER's sequence; day/week = ITEM-STATE schedules; months = PLACE. The identities are not modes; they are the five components seen from five angles.

## 2. PART 1 — THE VOCAL ACTION GRAMMAR

### 2.1 Reduction

Thirteen discovered verbs, reduced by asking "what does the learner do with the voice, and which latent construct (L1–L4) or boundary condition does the action load?":

| Discovered verb | Reduction | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| LEAP, SETTLE | phases of **FIND** | arrival and settling are two phases of producing an externally specified pitch; onset shape is research-only (H005), so the phases are not separately scored |
| HOLD | **HOLD** (distinct) | sustaining under a moving context loads feedback use (L3) and, if H013 holds, intentional stability; distinct information: the context changes, the target does not |
| STEP | **STEP** (distinct) | target defined relative to the learner's own current note; absolute and relative dissociate (C011) |
| KEEP, RETURN | **KEEP** with filler modifier | both reproduce a target after its reference is gone; RETURN's "delay" is filled with the learner's own excursion (interference); construct L1 in both |
| MIRROR | **MIRROR** (distinct) | reproduce an ordered gesture; loads L4 + L1 + relation; the "word" level |
| ANSWER (+ transform, complete, reply) | **ANSWER** with constraint modifier | produce a gesture that is *not* a copy, under a frame; generative under constraint; loads L4 and tonal expectation (C043) |
| LOCK | **FIND** or **STEP** with REF = present and CONSEQUENCE = fusion | same production operation, different sensing loop (beats/ring, L3 rather than L1/L2); a *condition* (H006/E-E), not a primitive |
| CALL-IT | **JUDGE** (distinct) | the output is a judgement, not a sound; loads perception/metacognition (C004/C010) |
| SCOUT | **ROAM** (distinct) | production with no target; success is coverage; loads boundary conditions (range, register), never a latent skill |
| DARK, REWIND | not actions: **item-state operator** and **recovery operator** | they modify the attempt; the learner does nothing different with the voice |

Result: **eight primitives** — FIND, HOLD, STEP, KEEP, MIRROR, ANSWER, JUDGE, ROAM — and a modifier set:

- REF ∈ {present (continuous), initial (heard once), named (cue only), none}
- FB ∈ {consequence (musical/world), terminal-coarse (informational), corrective (one directional cue), none, reflective (later)}
- DELAY ∈ {0, 1, 3, 5, 10, 30 s, session, day, week}; FILLER ∈ {silence, non-pitch task, distractor tone, own excursion}
- CONTEXT ∈ {key, register, vowel, tempo, backing texture, reference timbre}
- ITEM-STATE ∈ {onsight, flash, redpoint} × {lit, dark} × {due, not due}
- RECOVERY ∈ {rewind, continue, stop}

Which are genuinely distinct learning operations: FIND (external target, present or remembered briefly), STEP (relative target), KEEP (delayed target), HOLD (target under perturbation), MIRROR (ordered targets), ANSWER (constrained generation), JUDGE (perception of own or other output), ROAM (no target). FIND with REF=present (LOCK) and KEEP with FILLER=own excursion (RETURN) are variants that matter *experimentally* (they change the information condition) but not as separate things the voice does.

### 2.2 The canonical grammar

Every primitive specified with the ten required fields. "Coarse band" means ≥25 c from the settled center (C033 lay threshold; H007 sensitivity), never narrower.

**FIND** — produce the pitch of an external target.
- ACTION: leap from silence or from another note to a target region; settle.
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: the target (present, heard once, or named) and own voice.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: consequence on arrival (chord blooms / resonator wakes / partner answers); nothing before arrival except, optionally, one directional cue after 3 s of searching.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: the consequence does not occur; the world states the *relation* produced (the chord that contains what you sang; the neighbouring resonator that woke) — informational, not corrective.
- NEXT ACTION: try again from the current note (no reset), or STEP by the heard relation.
- MEASUREMENT: settled center vs target (coarse category); time-to-band (coarse); onset shape stored, unscored.
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: L2 (map) when REF=none/named; L1 when REF=initial; L3 when REF=present.
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as an action and as coarse measurement; EXPERIMENTAL as a *training* driver under any specific REF regime (H006).
- TRANSFER TARGET: untrained targets in the same register (near); other register/vowel (far, unknown).
- RETENTION TEST: the same target asked onsight (REF=none, FB=none until done) next day.

**HOLD** — stay on a pitch while the context moves.
- ACTION: sustain 5–30 s while harmony, a second voice or a drone moves; keep the voice where it is.
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: own voice against a changing context; optional faint cue of the held target.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: consonance/thickness of the context tracks whether the voice is still inside the band; on return of the harmony to the tonic, resolution occurs only if the voice is still there.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: the harmony does not resolve; the direction of the excursion is audible (the bass sits above or below you); no number.
- NEXT ACTION: re-settle (FIND from the current note back into the band) and continue holding.
- MEASUREMENT: time inside band; voiced coverage; excursion direction; nothing about spread.
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: L3 (feedback use under interference); H013 (intentional stability) — hypothesis.
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as a game event; EXPERIMENTAL as a skill claim (H013, FW15); the moving context is a continuous reference — an E-E condition.
- TRANSFER TARGET: holding under a different context (new progression, new register).
- RETENTION TEST: hold the same target under a *novel* context next day; compare time-in-band descriptively.

**STEP** — move from the current note by a named relation.
- ACTION: from wherever the voice is, move up/down by a degree or interval category ("up a third", "to 5").
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: the learner's own current note (always), the home (heard once or remembered), the relation name.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: the chord that contains the relation actually produced is played (a major third produces a major triad; a minor third a minor one; a tritone a diminished one) — the relation is *revealed by harmony*.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: the learner hears a different chord quality than intended; the category is audible, the cents are not.
- NEXT ACTION: adjust by a category (not by cents), or STEP again from the new note.
- MEASUREMENT: settled center(n) − settled center(n−1), categorical with ±50 c ambiguity handling (firewall).
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: relative pitch (unassigned between L2 and L4); C037 categorical perception; C029 interval deviation.
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as action and descriptive measurement; EXPERIMENTAL as training (consistent vs variable, H008; C025 single old study).
- TRANSFER TARGET: same relation from a new start (transposition); new relations (untrained categories).
- RETENTION TEST: relations asked from a remembered home, onsight, next day.

**KEEP** — reproduce a target after its reference is gone.
- ACTION: hear (or find) a note; wait through a delay and a filler; sing it back.
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: nothing during the delay except the filler; own voice at reproduction.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: on reproduction inside the band, the reference returns and joins the voice (unison or an added fifth); outside, the reference returns *where it was* and the learner hears the gap as a relation.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: the gap is heard; the item's delay schedule does not expand.
- NEXT ACTION: FIND the returned reference (immediate correction is part of the loop, not a penalty), then KEEP again at the same delay later.
- MEASUREMENT: settled center vs reference at delay d; bias and precision across repeats; the decline curve (C014).
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: L1 (representational precision); anchors (C013) at day-scale delays.
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as measurement (C014 supports the phenomenon); EXPERIMENTAL as training ("does practising KEEP change L1?" — unknown); the retention probe itself is SAFE.
- TRANSFER TARGET: untrained targets at the same delay; longer delays; different reference timbre (C047).
- RETENTION TEST: it is one — KEEP with DELAY=day and REF=none is the E-A probe.

**MIRROR** — reproduce an ordered gesture.
- ACTION: hear a 2–5-note gesture; sing it back, relative pitch, same contour.
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: the gesture (once, or on request); own voice.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: the partner sings back *what the learner actually sang* (cleaned resynthesis or nearest categorical rendering) followed by the model — the difference is heard as two versions, not stated.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: the learner hears where the shapes diverge; the partner shortens the gesture or repeats it (assistance returns), never says "wrong".
- NEXT ACTION: MIRROR the shortened gesture; or STEP the one relation that diverged.
- MEASUREMENT: per-note settled centers relative to the first note; contour agreement; interval categories; span reached.
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: L4 + L1 (span, C009); relative pitch; interval compression as a descriptive pattern (C006).
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as action and measurement; EXPERIMENTAL as a route to phrases (H003/FW6).
- TRANSFER TARGET: untrained gestures of the same length; longer gestures; transposed gestures.
- RETENTION TEST: yesterday's gesture asked by name (onsight of a redpoint); span retest on new gestures.

**ANSWER** — produce a gesture that is not a copy, under a frame.
- ACTION: hear a question (or a fragment); sing a completion, a reply that ends on a requested degree, or a transformation (same shape from a new start; change one note).
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: the question, the tonal frame (a sounding or remembered home), the constraint.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: the accompaniment cadences if the answer meets the frame (ends inside the band of the requested degree); otherwise it hangs, and the partner re-asks with the frame made more audible (the home sounds).
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: tension persists (musical), the frame becomes more present (assistance returns); the body of the answer is never judged.
- NEXT ACTION: ANSWER again; or STEP to the requested degree from where the answer ended.
- MEASUREMENT: final settled center vs frame; contour descriptively; for transformations, which note changed and whether the rest was reproduced.
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: L4 + tonal expectation (C043, C044); improvisational control (unknown construct).
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as interaction; BLOCKED for any rating of the answer's musical quality (FW16, C031); EXPERIMENTAL as a route to improvisation.
- TRANSFER TARGET: unfamiliar questions; new frames (keys); new constraint types.
- RETENTION TEST: the same question answered onsight after a week (the ending relation is the only thing compared).

**JUDGE** — perceive rather than produce.
- ACTION: (a) predict own outcome before the reveal (high/low/on; which note was off); (b) choose between two versions of own take; (c) classify a heard relation.
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: own memory of what was sung; or two stimuli; or one stimulus.
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: the reveal is about the *judgement* (agreed / disagreed), delivered as a coarse category; for (b) the chosen version plays with the model.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: nothing musical; a disagreement is logged and, if repeated, the partner offers the two-version comparison (b) for that item.
- NEXT ACTION: the production action the judgement was about, repeated.
- MEASUREMENT: agreement matrix (declared × measured category), base-rate corrected; discrimination thresholds (never below ≈25 c for learners).
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: perception (C004, C010, C015–C017); perception/production dissociation (C005); H010 (what learners hear as "on").
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE as a logged task (§9 safe-to-build: judgment tasks); BLOCKED as a claim that perception practice improves production (FW9, C016).
- TRANSFER TARGET: judgements on untrained items and unfamiliar timbres.
- RETENTION TEST: agreement and thresholds re-measured blind after a week.

**ROAM** — sing with no target.
- ACTION: explore; slide; visit edges; hold anywhere; play the instrument.
- INFORMATION AVAILABLE: own voice and the consequence renderer (harmony follows; rooms ring).
- SYSTEM RESPONSE: consequence only; the map fills in; nothing is evaluated.
- WHAT CHANGES ON FAILURE: there is no failure; edges are drawn, never rewarded.
- NEXT ACTION: anything; ROAM feeds targets to the partner (a place visited becomes a candidate item).
- MEASUREMENT: visited regions, durations, self-reported comfort; range/register metadata.
- POSSIBLE LEARNING CONSTRUCT: none claimed; boundary conditions; wide-range exposure has one immediate-effect study (C018).
- SCIENTIFIC STATUS: SAFE.
- TRANSFER TARGET: n/a.
- RETENTION TEST: "is the island still where you left it?" — a visited anchor re-found without reference (C013-type anchor stability; descriptive).

### 2.3 Composition rules

- A **problem** is a sequence of primitives under one modifier set: `KEEP(3 s, silence) → FIND → HOLD(6 s, harmony moves) | REF=initial, FB=consequence`.
- A **probe** is a problem with REF=none, FB=none-until-done, ITEM-STATE=due, on an item that is either trained (retention) or untrained (transfer). Probes are posed by the partner like any problem; the only difference the learner sees is that the partner "does not sing it first".
- A **session** is a partner's sequence of problems drawn from item states (due → new → practised), with ROAM allowed anywhere.
- Nothing in the grammar can express "score = cents". The finest learner-facing category is a coarse band or an interval category; the finest consequence is a chord quality or a room's ring.

Confidence: the eight-primitive reduction is a design judgement (M); the SAFE/EXPERIMENTAL/BLOCKED labels follow the canonical base directly (H).

## 3. PART 2 — FOUR COMPETING ARCHITECTURES

Each is a coherent product, not a theme. They differ in which component of the attempt is *primary* (drives what happens next) and which are subordinate. The eleven explanation points are given per architecture; loops are shown at six timescales.

### 3.1 Architecture A — INSTRUMENT-FIRST ("The Band That Follows You")

Primary component: CONSEQUENCE. The voice is an instrument inside a generative ensemble; control produces music; there are no items by default.

- **30 s:** ROAM/HOLD. Sing anything; the band harmonises; when the voice settles ≥1 s the harmony *locks* (a chord forms under it); a leap cues a chord change; a held note becomes a pedal while the band moves around it (HOLD under perturbation). Loop: sing → the band's response → hear whether it locked → settle or move → sing.
- **3 min:** a "set" — a chord map the band wants to visit; the learner steers it by holding roots; consistency across bars thickens the arrangement (layers agree → chorus); inconsistency thins it.
- **15 min:** three sets in different keys/registers; a "silent set" once per session where the band drops out for 8 bars and the learner must keep the root alone (HOLD/KEEP with REF=none) — the only probe.
- **First day:** discover that holding produces harmony and leaping steers it; record a set.
- **First week:** sets in more keys; silent sets lengthen; the learner's recorded sets accumulate (musical history).
- **First month:** the learner has a repertoire of steered progressions; silent sets reveal, descriptively, which roots return unaided.

What the learner does: plays. Why another attempt: the music is worth making; a set is never the same. Error: a note outside the band means the band harmonises *where you are* (never wrong, but the intended progression drifts); the world bends toward you. Feedback: consequence only. Assistance disappears: the band thins on schedule (M33 style) and drops out in silent sets. Progression: repertoire and silent-set capability (descriptive). After novelty: depends entirely on the musical quality of the generative band. Musicality: maximal. Retention: silent sets on roots used yesterday (E-A-compatible but items are learner-chosen, so overlap is uncontrolled). Phrases/songs/improv: native for improvisation; weak for scripted phrases and songs (no items). Hypotheses depended on: none for play; H006 (continuous reference during play) if any learning is claimed; H013 for holding.

**Red team A.** 20 sessions: yes if the band is good; no if it is a chord-follower (then it is M20, a toy). Meter-following: none (there is no meter). Performance vs learning: nothing is called learning, but nothing is *measured* either unless silent sets are formalised — then they are the item spine in disguise. Gaming: singing only comfortable roots; the band forgives everything. Real musical experience: the strongest of the four. Unsupported assumptions: none required; the danger is the opposite — it proves nothing. Progression = capability or difficulty? Neither; it is repertoire. **Fatal weakness:** a generative band that is musically satisfying is the hardest technical system in the whole programme, and the architecture's value is proportional to it. **Salvageable part:** the consequence renderer (lock/thin/steer) is required by every other architecture.

### 3.2 Architecture B — CONVERSATION-FIRST ("The Creature / The Answering Machine")

Primary component: PARTNER. A contingent musical agent poses everything; items are the agent's "words"; the loop is call and response.

- **30 s:** the partner sings a tone or a 2–3-note word; the learner MIRRORs it; the partner replies with what it heard (the learner's actual contour, rendered) and then repeats the model if they differ; if they match, the partner mutates the word (ANSWER/transform) and the exchange continues. Loop: hear → sing → hear yourself through the partner → decide (which note diverged) → sing again or accept the mutation.
- **3 min:** an exchange of 6–10 turns around one word and its mutations; the partner ends by asking a question phrase that needs an ANSWER ending on home.
- **15 min:** three words (one new, one from yesterday asked cold — the partner "forgets" it and asks you, i.e. an onsight probe — one practised); a free dialogue at the end where the partner only keeps the frame.
- **First day:** two words; the partner learns your comfortable register (ROAM as calibration, cf. One Hand Clapping).
- **First week:** a vocabulary of 6–8 words; question/answer; the partner asks for yesterday's words each morning.
- **First month:** words combine into phrases; transformations; the partner's requests span a week ("the word from Monday").

What the learner does: converses. Why another attempt: the partner responds, and the exchange goes somewhere. Error: the partner sings back what you did; the difference is a *second voice*, not a verdict; if the difference persists the partner shortens the word (assistance returns). Feedback: musical (contingent reply) + informational (the mirrored contour). Assistance disappears: the partner stops modelling first (flash → onsight), lengthens the delay before asking, and hides the home. Progression: vocabulary and what the partner can ask cold. After novelty: depends on the partner's generative range (mutations, questions) — a motif bank with transformations is enough for months if words are learner-influenced. Musicality: high (dialogue), but harmonic richness is limited unless A's renderer is added. Retention: the partner asks cold — the probe *is* the conversation's natural "do you remember?". Phrases/songs/improv: native path (word → sentence → dialogue); songs enter as "long words" with gaps (Farnsworth). Hypotheses: L4 relations (unknown), contingency as a driver in adults (unknown; C050 caution on self-triggered references), H003 for phrase growth.

**Red team B.** 20 sessions: yes if the partner's questions and mutations remain surprising; the creature identity (R02) sustains care. Meter-following: none; but the *mirrored contour* is a display — if rendered as a curve on screen it becomes a meter (keep it audible). Performance vs learning: the partner asking cold is honest; the risk is the partner's "recognition" being read as mastery — the partner must never say "you know this word", only ask. Gaming: singing words in the same register always; the partner must transpose. Real musical experience: yes, if the partner has taste (curated motif bank). Unsupported assumptions: none if unscored; the contingency threshold is a feel question (K4). Progression: vocabulary is capability-shaped (untrained items appear naturally), but *what the partner asks* must be driven by item state, not by streaks. **Weakness:** one-mic, one-voice; social variants (relay, SKATE) are later. **Strength:** it scales from a tone to a phrase without changing the loop, and every cold request is an experiment.

### 3.3 Architecture C — MEMORY/RETURN-FIRST ("The Lighthouse and the Rooms")

Primary component: ITEM-STATE. Everything is about returning to what was found: the item's state and delay schedule decide what is asked; the renderer is the Ringing Room.

- **30 s:** FIND → KEEP. A resonator hums faintly (initial reference); the learner wakes it (it rings and the hum stops); 3 s of silence; the learner wakes it again unheard; the room keeps ringing if both wakes agree. Loop: hear → find → wait → find again → hear the room's answer → next delay.
- **3 min:** a room of five resonators; each is found, kept at 3 s, then 10 s while another resonator is worked (interference), then 30 s; a resonator kept at 30 s stays "lit" for the session.
- **15 min:** two rooms (one in yesterday's key, one new); opening block: "wake what you can" in yesterday's room with no hum (blind, ≤60 s); then guided finds; CALL-IT after each keep (optional).
- **First day:** one room; learn that resonators ring and stay lit; the lighthouse ritual is introduced (home note).
- **First week:** lit resonators reappear; the home note is asked nightly; rooms in two keys; dark resonators (redpoint-with-hum-off) accumulate.
- **First month:** the map shows which rooms stay lit across days and which fade; new rooms open only when three old resonators can be woken unheard.

What the learner does: finds, keeps, returns. Why another attempt: the room is alive and dims; keeping it lit is a goal that spans days; the lighthouse is a ritual. Error: the wrong resonator wakes (the relation actually produced is heard); or nothing wakes (silence is information); a directional warmth (warmer/colder) is available on request. Feedback: consequence (ring, persistence) + sparse informational. Assistance disappears: hum → no hum, per resonator, earned by feedback-off repeats and reversible (M27), plus scheduled delay growth. Progression: which rooms and how long they stay lit under less support — capability under less support by construction. After novelty: rooms and keys vary; the home ritual is short; risk of feeling like a memory test if the renderer is thin. Musicality: medium — resonators can be scales/chords, so a lit room is a chord; but no partner, no phrase. Retention: native (the whole product is a delay ladder); E-A is the opening block. Phrases/songs: weak natively — a "room" is a set of pitches, not a sequence; phrases require B's partner or Farnsworth gaps on a melody rendered as a corridor of resonators. Hypotheses: H002/H009 (what the product *measures*, not assumes); H006 (hum on/off is an assigned condition); H010 (what "wakes" a resonator is f0-based; perceived center is open).

**Red team C.** 20 sessions: risk of monotony — rooms must vary in key, register, chord type, and the partner-less loop can feel like a lab unless the ring is beautiful (K1). Meter-following: low (no numbers), but resonator bandwidth is a hidden band — designers must not narrow it (FW1 creep). Performance vs learning: the strongest of the four — lit/dark states are feedback-off, and the opening blind block is the outcome. Gaming: harmonics wake resonators (octave/fifth exploits); fixed by weighting the fundamental and logging octave hits. Real musical experience: only if the room is musical (chords, timbre) — a prototype question. Unsupported assumptions: none; it *tests* the retention hypotheses. Progression: represents retained capability under less support, honestly. **Weakness:** no path to phrases or improvisation without importing B. **Strength:** it is the only architecture whose native currency is the missing data.

### 3.4 Architecture D — WORLD/EXPLORATION-FIRST ("Sonic Cartography")

Primary component: PLACE. A persistent map; places have pitches; travel is singing; fog is forgetting; regions open on blind returns.

- **30 s:** ROAM to a place (FIND its pitch; the place hums faintly if close — warmer/colder); arrive and stay 2 s (SETTLE/HOLD) to "land"; the place is drawn. Loop: sing → the map responds (warmth, arrival) → decide direction → sing.
- **3 min:** a journey between three places (STEP between known pitches); passages are drawn; a place revisited within a minute stays vivid.
- **15 min:** a region; a return trip to yesterday's places (fog lifts only if you arrive unaided — KEEP at day-scale); a new region opens when three old places are returned to unaided (knowledge gate).
- **First day:** discover home and two places; the map begins.
- **First week:** a region of 6–8 places in one key; the first fog; the first gate.
- **First month:** several regions (keys/registers); passages show the intervals the learner uses; fog shows what has faded; the map is the ledger.

What the learner does: travels by voice. Why another attempt: the map, the fog, unexplored regions. Error: you land somewhere else (the place you actually reached is drawn — navigation, not failure); a passage is recorded where you actually went. Feedback: world consequence + warmth. Assistance disappears: hums fade on places you have returned to; fog returns on places not revisited. Progression: territory retained under less support; gates are blind returns. After novelty: once a region is mapped the loop is "maintain the map" — sustaining only if regions keep opening (content) and if returning is pleasurable (musical rendering of arrival). Musicality: low unless places are musically meaningful (chords, rooms) — which is C. Retention: native (fog = 7 d, gate = 24 h), item registry = places. Phrases/songs: passages are intervals; journeys are sequences; songs could be routes — plausible but it is MIRROR in disguise. Hypotheses: same as C plus a design assumption that spatial metaphors make return desirable (unknown; prototype).

**Red team D.** 20 sessions: yes for explorers; no for those who want to sing music rather than travel. Meter-following: none. Performance vs learning: honest (fog and gates are blind). Gaming: staying in one region; landing on easy places; fixed by gates requiring old places across regions. Real musical experience: the weakest of the four — a map is not music unless the places are; the risk is lore. Unsupported assumptions: none scientific; one design assumption (spatial framing) that is untested. Progression: capability under less support (fog/gates), but the *build* is the largest. **Weakness:** first-day loop is slow (nothing to see until places exist), and the world is only as meaningful as the ledger it renders. **Strength:** it is the best long-term view of the item spine; as a *visualisation* of C it costs little.

### 4.2 The difficulty space

An item's difficulty is a vector, not a level:

```
D(item) = ⟨ REF, DELAY, FILLER, LENGTH, RANGE, NOVELTY, CONTEXT, TEMPO, REGISTER, TIMBRE, MODE ⟩
   REF      ∈ {present, initial, named, none}
   DELAY    ∈ {0, 1, 3, 5, 10, 30 s, session, day, week}
   FILLER   ∈ {silence, task, tone, own}
   LENGTH   ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}
   RANGE    ∈ {≤P5, ≤P8, >P8}
   NOVELTY  ∈ {trained, near, far}
   CONTEXT  ∈ {same key/backing, new key, new backing}
   TEMPO    ∈ {free, slow, musical, fast}
   REGISTER ∈ {comfortable, edge}
   TIMBRE   ∈ {piano, synthetic, voice-like}
   MODE     ∈ {FIND, HOLD, STEP, KEEP, MIRROR, ANSWER(complete|transform|reply)}
```

Rules:
1. **Tolerance is not a coordinate.** The band is a fixed policy (≥25 c) logged per version; E-F may change the policy later, never per learner.
2. **One coordinate moves at a time** per item (dog-training proofing rule, M34). An item that is *due* is asked at its current vector; success on feedback-off repeats permits one coordinate to move; failure reverts that coordinate (reversible, M27).
3. **The learner's progression is the frontier**, not a scalar: the set of vectors at which items are lit/redpointed under REF=none. Displayed as territory (§7), never as a level.
4. **Some coordinates are assigned, not earned.** REF regime, fading regime and grain are experimental conditions assigned per item (§8); the learner experiences them as "this room hums, that one does not".
5. **Game difficulty** (TEMPO, LENGTH, CONTEXT texture, harmonic movement in HOLD) may be raised freely by the partner for variety; **training difficulty** (REF, DELAY, NOVELTY) moves only by the rules above; **measured performance** never unlocks anything; **retention/transfer events** unlock regions.

Illustration: an item born as `⟨initial, 0, silence, 1, ≤P5, trained, same, free, comfortable, piano, FIND⟩` reaches, after weeks, `⟨none, day, own, 1, ≤P8, trained, new key, musical, comfortable, piano, KEEP⟩` — the same note, asked cold, tomorrow, after singing elsewhere, in a new key — with the band unchanged throughout.

Confidence in the space as a *structure*: H (it only encodes what the canonical base allows); confidence that its coordinates order difficulty *for learning*: L–M (that is what the assigned arms measure).

## 5. PART 4 — HELP SHOULD DISAPPEAR

Principle adopted: progression = capability under less support. What is removed is *information about the target*; what is never removed is *the musical consequence of what was produced*. That single rule preserves pleasure while support fades.

### 5.1 The proposed ladder, examined

```
hear + see → hear → brief cue → cue disappears → recall after delay → recall after interference → recall tomorrow → apply in new context → respond creatively
```

Three problems: (1) "see" is a concurrent visual display and belongs at the bottom only as an acquisition aid (C019–C021 immediate gains; C020 in-task dip; C028 no retention data) — it is a *condition*, not a rung; (2) the ladder conflates three axes (REF, DELAY, CONTEXT) into one order, which is exactly the H004/H008 question (context-first vs reference-first), so the order must be assignable; (3) "respond creatively" is a MODE change, not a support level.

### 5.2 Assistance states (support decreases downward), with what each removes

| State | REF | FB | What the learner has | What remains musical |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S0 doubled | present, continuous | consequence + (optional) concurrent visual | the target sounds with them; a corridor may be shown | the doubled tone itself |
| S1 heard | initial (once) | consequence + one directional cue on request | the target, then silence | the bloom on arrival |
| S2 named | named (degree/word) | consequence | only a name and the remembered home | the bloom; the chord that reveals the relation |
| S3 cold | none | consequence after the attempt | nothing but the request | the room answers *after* |
| S4 delayed | none + DELAY (3–30 s) | consequence after | the request and time | same |
| S5 interfered | none + DELAY + FILLER=own | consequence after | the request, time, and their own excursion | same |
| S6 tomorrow | none + DELAY=day | consequence after | the request across sleep | same |
| S7 transferred | none + NOVELTY/CONTEXT | consequence after | a new key, register or vowel | same |
| S8 generative | none + MODE=ANSWER | consequence (frame cadence) | a frame instead of a target | resolution |

S0 is a sandbox/acquisition state and is never required; a learner may start at S1. S8 is not "more removed" than S7; it is a different mode available from S2 onward.

### 5.3 Alternative fading structures (assignable arms)

- **F-earned (Rocksmith):** an item moves down one state after k coarse successes on *feedback-off* repeats at its current state; moves up one state after m failures. Reversible, per item. Hypothesis: H009 (fading helps) with FW4/FW5 explicitly avoided by the feedback-off criterion.
- **F-scheduled (assist-as-needed):** the REF strength (hum loudness, cue duration) decays continuously with time-on-item regardless of outcome; re-strengthens on sustained failure. Hypothesis: H009 in its scheduled form.
- **F-interleaved:** every session mixes states for the same item (S1, S3, S4 in random order), no monotone ladder. Hypothesis: variability (H008) applied to support rather than to targets.
- **F-context-first:** CONTEXT and TIMBRE vary *before* REF is removed (proof with support, then remove support). Hypothesis: H004 (context specificity) — if learning binds to context, varying early prevents binding.
- **F-reference-first:** REF is removed before any context variation (the proposed ladder). Hypothesis: the opposite ordering.
- **F-chosen:** the learner chooses the state (onsight attempts are precious, M30). Note: self-controlled *feedback* ≈0 in limb tasks (C050); choice here is about *attempt condition*, which affects engagement and yields honest labels; no learning claim.
- **F-constant:** support never fades (control arm). Required for E-A.

These are arms, assigned per item or per room, invisible to the learner as arms ("this room hums; that one never did").

### 5.4 Preserving musical pleasure while fading

- Fade the *guide*, keep the *consequence*: at S3–S7 the room still rings, the chord still blooms, the partner still answers — only *before* the attempt is there silence.
- Fade *visually first, audibly second*: any corridor disappears before the hum does (avoids visual dependency; anti-pattern 6).
- Silence before an attempt is framed as *anticipation* (the partner "waits for you"), not as withdrawal.
- A returned assistance (state moves up) is rendered as the partner "reminding" — a musical event, not a demotion notice.
- The harmony under a HOLD is *not* assistance; it is the perturbation, and it stays.

## 6. PART 5 — MAKE ERROR PLAYABLE

### 6.1 Five kinds of feedback and where each belongs

| Kind | What it says | Where it belongs | Firewall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corrective | what to change (direction, "earlier", "hold longer") | only on request, or after N misses, one cue at a time | never a magnitude |
| Informational | what happened, coarsely (which relation was produced; which note diverged) | terminal, after the attempt, as a musical rendering where possible | never cents; categories ≥25 c |
| Musical consequence | the sound the outcome makes (bloom/thin, ring, chord quality, partner's reply) | concurrent, default, always on | may be contingent on coarse events, never graded |
| World consequence | where the outcome put you (the place you actually reached; the room that woke) | session/day scale; the map | descriptive only |
| Reflective | what the history shows (ghosts, lit/dark, fog, teacher annotations) | after the session; on the map; teacher view | descriptive; teacher interprets |

The primary loop uses musical consequence; informational feedback closes the loop when consequence is ambiguous; corrective feedback is a resource the learner spends; world and reflective feedback are what the day and the map do with attempts.

### 6.2 Twelve loops where error stays informative and never punishes

Form: ACTION → FEEDBACK/CONSEQUENCE → INTERPRETATION → DECISION → NEXT ACTION. Each names its primitive, status and exposure.

**L1 · Bloom / Thin (HOLD; SAFE as event, EXPERIMENTAL as skill H013)**
Hold a note while the bass walks a progression → the chord is thick while the voice is inside the band and thins as it leaves; the bass sits audibly above or below the voice → "I drifted under it" → re-settle upward → the chord thickens; on the bass's return home, resolution happens only if the voice is there. Exposure: continuous reference (E-E condition).

**L2 · The chord that tells the relation (STEP; SAFE)**
From the current note, "go up a third" → the system plays the triad containing the interval *actually* produced: major, minor, or diminished/augmented → "that was minor; I under-stepped" → step a category wider → the major triad sounds. No number ever; the error is a *quality*. Exposure: categorical boundaries near ±50 c handled by playing the ambiguous chord as a suspension.

**L3 · The neighbour wakes (FIND; SAFE)**
Aim at resonator 5 in a room → resonator 6 rings instead → "I'm a step high" → slide down until 5 rings and 6 dies → keep. Exposure: harmonics wake resonators (log octave hits; weight the fundamental).

**L4 · The partner sings what you sang (MIRROR; SAFE)**
Mirror a three-note word → the partner replies with the learner's actual contour (resynthesised, cleaned), then the model → "my middle note was low" → re-sing with the middle note raised → the two versions converge and the partner mutates the word. Exposure: rendering the learner's contour on *screen* would make a meter; keep it audible.

**L5 · Where you landed (FIND on the map; SAFE)**
Travel toward a known place by singing → the map draws the place you actually reached and the passage you actually made → "I'm on the shoal below the island" → STEP up by the heard relation → arrival; both places now exist on the map. Exposure: none; navigation replaces failure.

**L6 · Warmer / colder (FIND/KEEP; SAFE)**
Search for a hidden note → a single word or a rising warmth in the room's sound after each candidate → "warmer, keep going" → next candidate in the same direction → "there": the note blooms. Exposure: hidden band ≥25 c.

**L7 · The reference comes back where it was (KEEP; SAFE)**
Keep a note through 10 s → sing → the reference returns at its true pitch and sounds *with* the voice; the gap is heard as an interval (or as unison) → "I came back a bit high" → FIND the reference (an immediate correction that is part of the loop) → KEEP again later at the same delay. Exposure: reference timbre is a condition (C047).

**L8 · The ghost fades in (KEEP with self-reference; SAFE as instrument)**
Sing yesterday's phrase → the ghost of yesterday is silent while you are close and fades in when you diverge → "the ghost appeared on the third note" → re-sing the third note's relation → silence returns. Exposure: audible ghost = continuous reference, so the fade-in threshold is a coarse band and the *silent* run is the probe.

**L9 · Rewind and heal (MIRROR/long sequence; SAFE)**
Sing a long phrase with the partner → a coarse miss makes the partner honk once and the music steps back one bar → "that leap was too small" → re-enter from the last settled note → the phrase continues; rewinds appear on the map as a count with no adjective. Exposure: none.

**L10 · Call it (JUDGE; SAFE)**
Sing a note → before any reveal, declare high/low/on → the reveal concerns the declaration: "you said low; it was" or "you said on; it was low" → "I can't hear my own lowness on this vowel" → the partner offers two versions of the take → choose. Exposure: the "truth" is a coarse measured category; H010 caveat; categories ≥25 c.

**L11 · The unresolved question (ANSWER; SAFE as interaction, BLOCKED for rating)**
Hear a question → answer freely, ending anywhere → if the ending is inside the band of home the accompaniment cadences; otherwise it hangs and the home sounds faintly → "I ended on the fifth" → STEP down to home → cadence. The body of the answer is never judged. Exposure: FW16 is avoided by judging only the ending.

**L12 · The band goes where you are (ROAM; SAFE)**
Sing anything → the band harmonises the pitch actually sung; if the intended root was elsewhere the progression drifts → "I'm steering it somewhere else" → either follow the new key (discovery) or HOLD the intended root until the band comes back → the band re-centres. Exposure: none; drift is navigation.

Two rules across all twelve: the outcome is always rendered as *something that happened in the music*, and the interpretation is always the learner's. The system supplies a category or a consequence; it never supplies a verdict.

## 7. PART 6 — MUSIC AS LANGUAGE

Taken literally: a language is acquired by exchanging utterances that mean something to another party, with comprehension leading production, with a small vocabulary used at full speed from the start, and with memory tested by the conversation itself rather than by a quiz. The canonical base neither supports nor forbids this for pitch; it constrains what may be claimed (H003, FW6–FW9) and notes that speech HVPT (C049) is not pitch.

### 7.1 The language grammar mapped onto the vocal grammar

| Language act | Vocal primitive + modifier | What it asks of the learner |
|---|---|---|
| IMITATE | MIRROR (REF=initial) | reproduce a gesture |
| RECOGNIZE | JUDGE (classify) | say which word/relation was heard |
| RECALL | KEEP; MIRROR with REF=named | produce a word from its name after delay |
| ANSWER | ANSWER(reply) | produce a related gesture ending on the frame |
| COMPLETE | ANSWER(complete) | finish a fragment |
| TRANSFORM | ANSWER(transform) | same shape, new start; change one note |
| CALL | learner poses (SKATE role) | produce a gesture *you* can reproduce twice |
| RESPOND | ANSWER(reply) to a CALL | answer a peer or partner |
| ANTICIPATE | JUDGE(predict) + ANSWER(complete) | sing the continuation before it sounds (temporal occlusion) |
| IMPROVISE | ANSWER(reply) with frame only | free within a frame; only the ending is judged |

### 7.2 Is it a better architecture than isolated target-note practice?

Under the canonical base the answer must be split:

- *As an engagement architecture:* yes, with high confidence — every stage yields a new request from a partner, items grow into relations and phrases without changing the loop, and untrained items arise naturally.
- *As a learning architecture:* unknown. H003 says cross-grain transfer has not been detected under low dose and a unified account remains viable; H004 says learning may bind to items; C025 (old, single) favours consistent interval practice; C018 favours wide-range target variability for single notes (H008: task-dependent). Nothing says relational practice beats note practice for anything measured.
- *As a measurement architecture:* better, because the language path makes E-B's grain manipulation (notes vs 3–5-note phrases, dose and overlap controlled) a natural part of play rather than an added test.

Therefore the architecture includes both grains as **assigned arms** (words-first vs notes-first per room/creature), and the product never says that words teach songs.

### 7.3 The path

Stages are defined by *what the partner can ask* (item state), not by what the learner "has mastered". Each stage names the primitives, the item type, the consequence, and the untrained-item source.

**Stage 0 — Phoneme: a single pitch.** Primitives: FIND (S1), KEEP (S3–S6), HOLD (short), JUDGE(call it). Item: a resonator/place/tone. Consequence: ring/bloom. Untrained items: new resonators in the same room. Language analogue: a sound you can make on demand. Retention: the resonator asked cold tomorrow. Status: SAFE.

**Stage 1 — Syllable: a two-note relation.** Primitives: STEP; MIRROR(2). Item: a degree pair ("1–5", "1–3") — the Koch vocabulary of two. Consequence: the chord that reveals the relation. Untrained items: a third degree added when 2-note calls are consistent across ≥4 feedback-off repeats. Analogue: the first two words at full speed. Retention: the pair asked from a remembered home. Status: SAFE action; EXPERIMENTAL as training (H008).

**Stage 2 — Word: a short gesture.** Primitives: MIRROR(3–4); ANSWER(transform: transpose, mutate one). Item: a motif the partner owns ("rain", "door" — names are for the creature, never note names). Consequence: the partner sings back what you sang, then the model; mutations continue the exchange. Untrained: mutations and transpositions are new items by construction. Analogue: vocabulary. Retention: "what was the word for rain?" next day. Status: SAFE action; EXPERIMENTAL as a route to phrases (H003).

**Stage 3 — Sentence: question and answer.** Primitives: ANSWER(complete, reply); JUDGE(anticipate). Item: a question phrase with a frame. Consequence: cadence or suspension. Untrained: unfamiliar questions daily. Analogue: the first exchanges. Retention: the ending relation of the same question a week later. Status: SAFE interaction; BLOCKED for rating.

**Stage 4 — Utterance: a phrase.** Primitives: MIRROR(5+), KEEP through Farnsworth gaps, REWIND. Item: a phrase with gap structure (gaps shrink over days) and a dark/lit state per phrase. Consequence: the phrase reassembles; rewinds heal. Untrained: new phrases from the same vocabulary. Retention: dark phrases checked cold at the start of the next session. Status: EXPERIMENTAL (H009 for fading; H003 for grain).

**Stage 5 — Conversation.** Primitives: ANSWER(reply) chains; CALL/RESPOND with a peer (SKATE, relay). Item: chains, not fixed items. Consequence: the dialogue continues; chains are audible artefacts. Untrained: everything. Retention: a phrase from the chain returns after a week. Status: SAFE as interaction; social layer later.

**Stage 6 — Song / improvisation.** Two doors: (a) a known song as a *long utterance* reassembled through gaps (Farnsworth songs) with an unfamiliar-song probe assigned (E-B arm); (b) improvisation as ANSWER with frame only, earned by faithful imitation (M16). Consequence: the song completes; the band follows. Retention: the song's gap structure re-expanded after a week (does it still reassemble?). Status: EXPERIMENTAL; no transfer claim; the unfamiliar-song probe is the only honest indicator (C029 scoring: interval deviation and modulations).

The stages are not levels: a learner can be at Stage 3 on one word and Stage 0 on a new resonator; the partner asks according to item state. What the language framing changes is that the *unit of request* grows while the *loop* stays identical: hear → sing → hear the consequence → decide → sing.

## 8. PART 7 — THE WORLD LAYER

### 8.1 Does persistence add value?

Yes, under one condition: the world is the item ledger rendered spatially, and nothing else. Its value is that *returning* becomes desirable (a place to go back to) and *forgetting* becomes visible without shame (fog, not a red score). The six-way test for every persistent object: it must correspond to a capability, a memory, a discovered property of the learner's voice, a retained skill (as a blind event, never a label), an experiment (an assigned condition), or meaningful musical history. Objects that fail the test are lore and are cut.

### 8.2 Persistent objects that pass

| Object | Corresponds to | What it renders | State | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Home** (the lighthouse) | a memory + an experiment | the learner's personal anchor note, found cold nightly | lit if found within band unaided tonight; dim otherwise | SAFE (C013 coarse anchors exist; a daily blind series is an anchor-stability instrument) |
| **Rooms** (resonator sets) | items in a key/scale + an assigned REF condition | resonators as pitches; the room's chord when several are lit | each resonator: onsight/flash/redpoint × lit/dark × due; the room: hum on/off (assigned) | SAFE spine; EXPERIMENTAL condition (E-E) |
| **Passages** | a discovered property (intervals the learner makes) | drawn between places actually reached | width = how often; never a grade | SAFE (descriptive) |
| **Edges** | a discovered property (range) | the map's coastline | drawn from ROAM; never rewarded | SAFE (physiological caution) |
| **Fog** | a retained skill's absence | places whose last blind success is older than the fog time (7 d) | returns automatically; lifts only on a blind return | SAFE (it *is* the 7 d probe) |
| **Gates** | retained capability | a new region opens when three old places are returned to cold | — | SAFE (M31 rule) |
| **The creature** (partner) | items as words + memory | its vocabulary = the words with their states; it "forgets" on schedule and asks | per word: heard/known-with-cue/known-cold/due | SAFE spine; EXPERIMENTAL grain arm |
| **Echo cave** | musical history | the learner's takes as ghosts; yesterday's take can be a partner | per take: date, item, condition | SAFE (reflective) |
| **Choral hall** | an experiment | rooms where a held chord is the perturbation (HOLD) — always with the harmony as reference | assigned | EXPERIMENTAL (H006/H013) |
| **Weather** | a capability event | hold durations under perturbation, rendered as calm/storm | descriptive | SAFE event |
| **Dark phrases shelf** | items at Stage 4 with fading state | phrases whose guide has left | earned/scheduled (assigned) | EXPERIMENTAL (H009) |

Objects cut by the test: currencies, decorative biomes, character progression, streak flames, "levels" of any object, badges for accuracy, any object whose state is set by in-feedback performance.

### 8.3 How the world behaves

- The map fills from ROAM and from posed items; it never asks the learner to "unlock the map".
- Each morning the map shows what is *due* (fog approaching) and what the creature will ask; the learner may go anywhere else.
- Nothing on the map is a number. Fog, light, warmth, passages and coastlines are the only visual vocabulary; note names appear only on request (display, never verdict).
- The teacher's view (§10) is the same map with the ledger underneath: conditions, coarse outcomes, versioned centers, annotation slots.

## 9. PART 8 — THE INVISIBLE RESEARCH ENGINE

### 9.1 From ordinary play to data

| Data need | Produced by | Without the lab feel because… |
|---|---|---|
| 0 h / 24 h / 7 d retention | KEEP at session-, day- and week-scale delays; the creature's cold requests; fog; gates; the lighthouse | asking is what the partner and the world do; one to three cold requests per session, never at the end |
| trained vs untrained items | every mutation, new resonator, new degree, unfamiliar question, peer phrase | new items arrive as content, flagged in the registry |
| reference dependence | rooms assigned hum on / hum off; ghost silent / on-divergence / audible; choral hall vs quiet room | rooms differ in character, not in "condition" |
| feedback dependence | S0 sandbox vs S1–S3; dark vs lit phrases; earned vs scheduled fading assigned per item | the guide leaving is a per-phrase event |
| grain transfer | words-first vs notes-first creatures/rooms; unfamiliar-song probe as a "long utterance" | grain is what a partner *likes to ask* |
| bias vs precision | every FIND/KEEP repeated ≥4 times per item across days | repetition is the loop |
| absolute vs relational | FIND/KEEP (absolute) vs STEP/MIRROR (relative) on the same learner, logged separately | both are ordinary requests |

Logging schema (extends §9 safe-to-build of the canonical base): `attempt{item_id, primitive, modifiers(REF, DELAY, FILLER, LENGTH, RANGE, NOVELTY, CONTEXT, TEMPO, REGISTER, TIMBRE, MODE), assigned_arm, item_state_before/after, coarse_outcome, settled_span_ref, centers{uniform, median, recency, last200} (versioned), onset_class, judge{declared, measured}, device/headphones, ts}` plus `item{grain, familiarity, overlap_flags, first_seen, states_history}` and `probe{kind: retention|transfer, delay, blind: true}`.

### 9.2 Mapping onto the backlog

**E-A — retention of visual/concurrent-feedback gains (H002, H009).** Arms assigned per room: S0 sandbox available vs never; per phrase: F-earned vs F-scheduled vs F-constant. Outcome: the opening blind block ("wake what you can", dark-phrase check) at 24 h and the fog lift at 7 d, on trained *and* untrained (new resonator in the same room) items. Mechanics: Rooms, Dark phrases, Ghost silent, Lighthouse. Validity: blind block precedes any guided play; items registered; centers versioned.

**E-B — grain transfer with dose and overlap (H003, H004).** Arms: words-first vs notes-first creature; dose = attempts per item logged; overlap flags between words and the phrases built from them; the unfamiliar-song probe as a long utterance with gaps expanded. Mechanics: Two-note language, Creature words, Farnsworth songs, SKATE phrases. Validity: unfamiliar song scripted (not a relay phrase); visual off; terminal audio only.

**E-C — slider–voice decomposition (H001).** The creature can be taught a word by *dragging* its pitch (adjustment with concurrent comparison, own-voice timbre) or by *singing*; "the creature is far away" = masked own voice; "teach it later" = delay; same items in both channels. Mechanics: Creature (drag channel), Call it (perception side). Validity: six targets per condition, counterbalanced; slider uses the learner's recorded timbre (C005).

**E-G — real-voice perceived pitch (H010).** "Two versions of you" in the echo cave: resynthesised own notes (same settled/different scoop; same mean/different endpoint; vibrato asymmetry) — "which one is you?"; Call-it declarations vs the four center definitions computed in parallel. Mechanics: Echo cave, Call it, Beat rooms (with the f0 caveat). Validity: M1 capture is prerequisite; adjustment matching needs the drag channel.

**E-D — initial-pitch marker (H005).** Farnsworth gaps (prepared onsets) vs Flick requests (fast onsets) on the same targets and registers, with an explicit research variant of the instruction ("start exactly on it" vs "get there however you like"); onset class logged from capture. Mechanics: Farnsworth songs, Flick hunts. Validity: capture-resolved onsets; register/vowel logged; nothing learner-facing.

**Mechanics that double as experiments without modification:** the opening blind block (E-A), the creature's cold requests (E-A/E-B), rooms with and without hum (E-E), the drag channel (E-C), two versions of you (E-G), gap vs flick (E-D). None requires the learner to know an experiment exists; all require the registry and assignment to exist from MVP-0.

Rule kept: game feel never changes assignment, the timing of blind probes or item novelty; the learner chooses *content* (which room, which word), never *condition*.

## 10. PART 9 — THE MINIMUM LOVABLE CORE

Constraint honoured: everything below sits on the existing real-time YIN detector (30 Hz analysis, 85 ms window), the reference keyboard/drone, pitch history, Practice Mode and the ShortWindowPitchEvidence stack (settled center as duration-weighted median over a 500 ms trailing window; sufficiency ≥6 voiced samples and ≥300 ms; `projectEvidenceToTarget` giving a center offset). The one existing constant that must *not* be reused as a criterion is Practice Mode's ±10 c band; the calibration table already contains 25 and 50 c bands, and 25 c is the coarse band throughout.

The unique thesis the core must demonstrate: **a vocal action rendered as musical consequence, attached to an item that remembers, asked again later** — not a tuner, not a score.

### MVP-0 — "Keep and Call It" (1–2 days)

**Mechanics.** One primitive pair: KEEP + JUDGE, with FIND as entry. Five targets in the learner's comfortable range (chosen once by a 30-second ROAM: the app proposes five pitches inside the observed range). One target is designated *home*.
**Loop (30 s).** The keyboard sounds the target for 1.5 s → silence for d ∈ {0, 3, 10} s (drawn, not chosen) → the learner sings → the evidence settles (≥300 ms) → the learner taps one of three buttons: *low / on / high* → the reveal: the reference returns at its true pitch *together with* the sung note held by the drone (unison or the heard gap), and, if the sung center was inside ±25 c, a fifth is added (bloom); a one-line reveal about the *prediction* ("you said high — it was"; "you said on — it was a bit low"). Next target.
**Session (10 min).** Each target visited at d=0, then 3, then 10 s, interleaved; home is visited last at 10 s. Day 2: the session opens with *home asked cold* (no tone, no delay countdown — "sing home"), then the four others at flash (heard once) before any guided repeats.
**Required systems.** Existing: detector, evidence, keyboard, drone. New: a 60-line state machine (target → delay → listen → judge → reveal); a coarse reveal component with no cents (three words, a chord); attempt log in IndexedDB/localStorage with JSON export (`attempt{target, delay, declared, measuredCategory, centers{uniformMean, median, recency, last200}, sufficiency, headphones}`); a "home" flag.
**What NOT to build.** Any cents display in this mode; XP; resonators; a registry beyond five fixed targets; fading arms; the map; a partner; onboarding copy that says "train".
**Question it answers.** Does hear → wait → sing → predict → bloom hold attention for ten minutes and bring the learner back on day 2? Does the 25 c band feel like cause and effect? Does declared agree with measured beyond base rate (first perception/production agreement data)? What does the delay curve look like on our stack (C014-shaped or not)?
**Evidence collected.** Delay × bias/precision per target; agreement matrices; home-note stability across days (first anchor series); day-2 return; centers under four definitions (E-G groundwork).
**Why it is the beginning of the product.** The loop, the coarse band, the judgement, the logging schema and the "asked cold tomorrow" moment are all final. Only the renderer (a chord) is provisional.

### MVP-1 — "The Ringing Room" (about one week)

**Mechanics.** FIND, KEEP, HOLD (short), JUDGE (optional), ROAM, with the Ringing Room as renderer and items with state.
**Loop (30 s).** A room of five to seven resonators (scale degrees inside the calibrated range; one room per key). A resonator *hums* faintly (S1) or is silent (S3, assigned per room). The learner sings; when the settled center sits inside a resonator's band for ≥500 ms it *wakes* (its tone swells, with the fundamental weighted so harmonics do not trigger octave neighbours; octave hits are logged) and stays lit with a slow decay (≈60 s); waking it again before it fades resets the decay. A wrong resonator waking is the informational feedback (L3 of §6.2). Warmer/colder is available on a button.
**Session (12–15 min).** Scheduler draws from item states: due (lit yesterday, decayed) → new → practised. Delays grow per resonator (3 → 10 → 30 s, with another resonator worked in between as interference). One to three cold requests per session ("wake the fourth one" with nothing sounding), never at the end. Day ≥2 opens with a 60-second blind block in yesterday's room: "wake what you can". Home from MVP-0 becomes the lighthouse: asked cold at the end of the day (or start), lit/dim.
**Required systems.** Existing: as MVP-0 plus pitch history for the teacher view. New: resonator audio engine (Web Audio oscillator/partial stacks with envelopes; band membership from the evidence, not per-frame); item registry (`item{id, room, degree, state: onsight|flash|redpoint, lit, dark, dueAt, assignedRef}`); scheduler; blind-block controller; hum on/off assignment per room; **M1 capture** (raw per-frame JSONL with events, replay CLI) because everything after depends on it; export.
**What NOT to build.** The creature; phrases; the map (a list of rooms suffices); ghosts; social; any visible pitch trace in the room; adaptive band width.
**Question it answers.** K1: can real voices with vibrato and breath drive sound-consequence feedback usably? Does a lit room feel like a place worth keeping alive? Does per-item support removal (hum → silent) read as progression? First 24 h data on trained vs untrained resonators (E-A pilot infrastructure). Does hum-on vs hum-off differ in the blind block (E-E pilot, descriptive only at this N)?
**Evidence collected.** Everything in MVP-0 plus item-state histories, blind-block outcomes at 24 h (and 7 d for anyone who stays), room condition, capture files for later re-analysis with candidate center definitions and detector variants (M1b).

### MVP-2 — "Words and the Map" (about one month)

**Mechanics.** Adds STEP, MIRROR, ANSWER(complete, transform) and a partner; keeps the room as renderer; adds the map as ledger view; adds assigned fading and grain arms; adds ghosts.
**Loops.** *Syllable:* "from where you are, go to 5" → the triad containing the produced relation sounds (L2). *Word:* the partner (a motif bank of 2–4-note words in degrees, transposable, mutate-one) sings a word → the learner mirrors → the partner plays back the learner's take, then the model (L4; playback of the recording at first, cleaned resynthesis later) → mutation continues. *Sentence:* a question with a frame → the learner completes it → cadence or suspension (L11). Koch rule: a degree is added to the vocabulary when 2-note calls are consistent over ≥4 feedback-off repeats. Farnsworth gaps on one known melody as the first Stage-4 item (optional in month one).
**Assigned arms.** Per item: F-earned vs F-scheduled vs F-constant fading (E-A/H009). Per partner instance: words-first vs notes-first (E-B grain). Per room: hum on/off (E-E). Assignment is logged; the learner sees character, not conditions.
**Ghosts.** From capture: yesterday's take of a word as a silent or on-divergence partner (L8), silent by default.
**Map-lite.** Rooms, home, fog (7 d), lit/dark; passages from ROAM; no regions or gates yet.
**Teacher view.** The ledger as a table: items, states, conditions, coarse outcomes, versioned centers, annotation slots with rater ID; nothing rated.
**Required systems.** Motif bank and transformation rules; interval-category classifier with ambiguity handling (suspension chord near ±50 c); simple cadence/suspension renderer; recording playback; ghost divergence detector on settled centers; fog/due scheduler; export and a first analysis notebook for E-A pilot numbers.
**What NOT to build.** The generative band; social relay/SKATE; regions and gates; vibrato/onset/stability anything learner-facing; adaptive difficulty from measurements; ratings of answers; own-voice-timbre slider (the drag channel ships with a plain tone and is marked as not yet C005-equivalent).
**Question it answers.** Does the loop survive the jump from tone to word without changing character (the language thesis, engagement half)? Do cold requests feel like conversation or like a test (K4)? Is the interval-category reveal-by-chord legible (L2)? Pilot effect sizes and dropout for the three fading arms and two grain arms, to size E-A and E-B properly.
**Evidence collected.** Everything above plus grain-tagged items with overlap flags, dose per item, transformation outcomes, ghost divergence maps, first 7 d fog data.

Each MVP is a strict superset in *loop*, not a rewrite: MVP-0's state machine becomes one primitive of MVP-1's scheduler; MVP-1's rooms are the renderer for MVP-2's words.

## 11. PART 10 — SYNTHESIS

### 11.1 Product thesis

Vocal Tuner is a place where the voice is played, not measured. Every attempt is one of eight vocal actions — find, hold, step, keep, mirror, answer, judge, roam — posed by a musical partner or a place, rendered as a musical consequence rather than a number, attached to an item that carries an honest state (never heard, heard once, practised, dark, due), and remembered by a map on which what has faded is fog, not failure. Support disappears per item and reversibly; tolerance never tightens; nothing is called learned. The same daily asking that makes the world worth returning to — "can you wake yesterday's room, sing home, answer the creature cold?" — is the retention experiment the field has never run, so the product is a learning environment and a longitudinal laboratory by the same mechanism, and the tuner's precision stays underneath as description, versioned, for teachers and research.

### 11.2 Canonical Vocal Action Grammar — §2 (FIND, HOLD, STEP, KEEP, MIRROR, ANSWER, JUDGE, ROAM; modifiers REF, FB, DELAY, FILLER, CONTEXT, ITEM-STATE, RECOVERY; LOCK and RETURN as variants; DARK and REWIND as operators).

### 11.3 The four competing architectures — §3 (A Instrument, B Conversation, C Memory, D World).

### 11.4 Red-team results — §3.1–3.5. Summary: A proves nothing and is hardest to build; B scales best and is honest if the partner never certifies; C is the only one whose currency is the missing data; D is a view, not a product, until C exists. None requires an unsupported assumption when unscored; all four fail if any number becomes a verdict.

### 11.5 Multidimensional progression model — §4 (the vector D(item); tolerance not a coordinate; one coordinate at a time; frontier not level; assigned vs earned coordinates).

### 11.6 Assistance/fading model — §5 (S0–S8 states; F-earned, F-scheduled, F-interleaved, F-context-first, F-reference-first, F-chosen, F-constant as arms; fade the guide, keep the consequence).

### 11.7 Error-as-musical-consequence system — §6 (five feedback kinds and their places; twelve loops L1–L12).

### 11.8 Music-as-language architecture — §7 (grammar mapping; split verdict; Stages 0–6 by what the partner can ask; both grains as arms).

### 11.9 Persistent-world architecture — §8 (six-way test; Home, Rooms, Passages, Edges, Fog, Gates, Creature, Echo cave, Choral hall, Weather, Dark shelf; everything else cut).

### 11.12 What should explicitly NOT be built yet

1. Cents-tier scoring or any band narrower than 25 c that the learner can see (H007 unresolved; C033/C037).
2. Within-note "stability"/spread as a learner-facing quantity (no perceptual counterpart).
3. Onset/attack feedback (H005 demoted; E-D pending).
4. Vibrato scoring (descriptive only; ×0.71 attenuation; C035/C036).
5. Global singing scores, ranks, leaderboards (FW16; C011, C030, C031; detector-exploitation risk C054).
6. Mastery labels from any performance during feedback (FW4/FW5; C028).
7. A drone/continuous-reference curriculum, in either direction (FW10–13; C046; Graveyard) — rooms with hum are assigned conditions only.
8. Perception-only modules presented as production training (FW9; C016).
9. Adaptive difficulty driven by per-frame cents or by in-feedback performance (C054; anti-patterns 8, 16).
10. Promises that words/phrases teach songs (H003; FW6/FW7); the unfamiliar-song probe is the only honest statement.
11. Phenotype routing or "your type" (H011/H014 hypotheses).
12. Any learner-facing statement that a measured center is "the pitch you sang" (H010; E-G) — centers stay versioned and internal; categories must be robust across definitions or be treated as ambiguous.
13. The generative band (A) and the social layer — not because blocked, but because their value depends on prototype answers (K1, K4) and on the spine existing first.

### 11.14 Strongest unresolved architectural forks

1. **Fading regime** — earned/reversible per item vs scheduled vs interleaved (H009). Preserved as arms; decided by E-A.
2. **Grain** — words-first vs notes-first (H003/H004/H008). Preserved as arms; decided by E-B.
3. **Primary consequence renderer** — resonance (rooms) vs harmony-following (band) vs partner mirror-back. A prototype fork (K1): rooms first because cheapest and most measurable; band deferred.
4. **Poser** — partner (creature) vs place (map) as the thing that asks cold. Both render one ledger; decide by prototype preference (K4) in MVP-2; the ledger is identical either way.
5. **Default reference regime** when no arm is assigned — initial (S1) vs none (S3). Interim: S1 (the standard matching paradigm in C018/C019); E-E decides.
6. **Center definition** (H010) — duration-weighted median (robust, exists) vs recency-weighted (perceptually motivated, synthetic evidence only). Interim: median internally, all four logged, categories must agree or be ambiguous; E-G decides.
7. **Band policy** hidden 25 vs 50 c (H007). Interim 25 c; E-F decides.
8. **Social timing** — SKATE/relay after the spine, not before; no evidence-based reason to hurry, one engagement reason to (peer-generated untrained items).
